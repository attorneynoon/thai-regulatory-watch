# TCCT Decision-Intelligence Case-Family Playbook

This playbook prevents a single competition-law template from flattening materially different rulings. It governs the fourth analysis layer; it does not replace statute-specific human review.

## 1. Unfair trade / Section 57 family

### Nature tags

- unilateral term or policy change;
- forced campaign participation or tying;
- discriminatory treatment;
- termination, suspension or refusal to deal;
- payment or credit terms;
- affiliated-service preference or self-preferencing;
- logistics/access restriction;
- information asymmetry or surprise change.

### Required legal/economic work

- identify the exact act, term or refusal;
- identify the case-specific unfairness benchmark rather than assuming dependency;
- compare similarly situated counterparties or alternative arrangements where relevant;
- test objective business justification and proportionality;
- trace concrete harm and causation;
- examine notice, consent, cure, exception and appeal paths;
- distinguish the claimant-specific failure of proof from any systemic practice not reached.

### Evidence depth

Actual evidence may include contracts, notices, tickets, opt-in/opt-out records, seller or counterparty communications, transaction logs, comparator treatment, financial-loss records and operational metrics.

Ideal evidence must state the element, custodian, native format, authentication method, expected rebuttal and why it could change the result.

Dependency, dominance, market definition and HHI are not universal Section 57 requirements. They become relevant only when the pleaded theory, applicable rule or a separately reached Section 50 issue makes them relevant.

## 2. Horizontal agreement / Section 54 family

### Nature tags

- price fixing;
- market/customer allocation;
- output restriction;
- bid rigging;
- information exchange;
- facilitated coordination.

### Required legal/economic work

- identify contact, agreement or concerted practice;
- separate direct from circumstantial evidence;
- classify object/effect and causal theory where legally relevant;
- map implementation, monitoring and attribution to each participant;
- assess withdrawal, limitation, settlement and ne bis in idem issues;
- distinguish legitimate parallel conduct from coordination.

Monitoring or enforcement can strengthen proof of an agreement or implementation. It is not automatically a universal statutory element.

## 3. Vertical agreement / Section 55 family

### Nature tags

- resale-price maintenance;
- exclusive dealing;
- MFN or parity clause;
- tying/bundling;
- territorial/customer restriction;
- platform rule imposed on dependent counterparties.

### Required legal/economic work

- establish the vertical relationship and exact restraint;
- determine the correct legal category;
- test coverage, duration, foreclosure and alternatives;
- test efficiencies, pass-through and less restrictive means;
- identify whether market power is legally/economically necessary for the theory used.

## 4. Abuse of dominance / Section 50 family

### Nature tags

- exclusionary abuse;
- exploitative term;
- discrimination;
- refusal to deal/access;
- self-preferencing;
- tying or leveraging;
- predation or margin squeeze.

### Required legal/economic work

- define the relevant market only to the degree required by the theory and record;
- establish market power separately from conduct;
- articulate the exclusionary or exploitative mechanism;
- trace foreclosure/harm and causation;
- evaluate efficiencies, objective necessity, proportionality and remedy.

The output must not reason that a firm is abusive merely because it is dominant, or dominant merely because the challenged conduct appears harsh.

## 5. Merger pre-approval / Sections 51(2) and 52 family

### Common core

- control, jurisdiction and thresholds;
- transaction perimeter and counterfactual;
- relevant overlaps and affected markets;
- market structure and concentration evidence;
- entry, expansion and buyer power;
- efficiencies, failing-firm claims and verifiability;
- Section 52 balancing;
- remedies, conditions, monitoring and enforceability.

### Horizontal merger

Analyze unilateral and coordinated effects, closeness of competition, diversion, capacity, bidding or differentiated-product evidence. Use HHI and delta only if the market, data and decision record support them; do not substitute HHI for competitive-effects analysis.

### Vertical merger

Analyze ability, incentive and effect for input or customer foreclosure; access to competitively sensitive information; raising rivals’ costs; elimination of double marginalization; and less restrictive remedies.

### Conglomerate/ecosystem merger

Analyze tying/bundling capability, portfolio effects, interoperability, data combination, switching costs, network effects, multi-homing, adjacent-market entry and innovation incentives.

### Distressed/failing-firm theory

Require evidence of imminent failure, absence of a less anticompetitive purchaser and the counterfactual disposition of assets or market share.

## 6. Merger post-notification / Section 51(1) family

### Required work

- control and transaction perimeter;
- applicable threshold;
- transaction date and notification deadline;
- filing completeness;
- relevant structural change and any findings the published decision actually makes.

Do not manufacture a full Section 52 authorization analysis merely because the source contains market-share information. Acknowledgement of a timely notification is not the same procedural act as pre-merger approval.

## 7. Cross-family hybrid handling

A platform case may implicate Section 57, Section 50, a vertical restraint and a merger/ecosystem theory at once. The router must still choose the primary family based on what was pleaded and decided, then preserve secondary families and nature tags. It must not collapse them into a generic “platform competition” conclusion.

## 8. Required litigation war-room questions

For every family:

1. What did each side actually argue?
2. Which evidence was actually mentioned or relied upon, and for what element?
3. What was accepted, rejected, untested or not reached?
4. What evidence would have been ideal, who holds it and how would it be authenticated?
5. What is the strongest counterargument and reply evidence?
6. Which missing fact would most likely change the outcome?
7. What can the claimant/regulator prove better?
8. What can the respondent defend better?
9. What can fairly be criticized in TCCT’s public reasoning, and what limits that criticism?
10. What operational evidence should a business preserve before the next dispute?
